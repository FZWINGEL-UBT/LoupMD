# Which training optimizations to try before Stage 1

This reviews the proposed accelerations against the [training-engine design](loup-s-training-engine.md). **Decision: diagnose fit, qualify precision, try existing backends, then profile the winner.** No new kernel, training recipe, precision switch, paid run or hardware adoption is approved by this review. Stage 0 stays on pinned mace-torch. Separate (1) seconds per identical update, (2) cost per qualified run and (3) MD speed of the shipped student. They have different denominators; do not multiply gains across them.

## What the actual workload rules out

**The first risk is unsuccessful training, not slow training.** On the frozen validation split the component RMSE of predicting zero forces is **651.29 meV/Å**. At epoch 129 the 64-wide radial-head seed reached **1,170.77**, versus 221.82 at epoch 30; the 8-wide seed reached **283.53** at epoch 105. The deterioration started *before* the phase-two loss change at epoch 111. One seed, unequal epochs, interim validation: neither a final student ranking nor proof that Adam is the cause. Preserve both scheduled runs. Audit physical E/F/stress labels through materialization (units, sign, shape, head, frame identity); compare train/validation by species, trajectory, temperature and strain, raw versus EMA and loss components around the first rise. Check a small training subset can be fitted before changing optimizers. Match isolated single-geometry teacher evaluations and paired energy/force or strain differences if a label mismatch is suspected. Never select a checkpoint or tune a fix on the fixed student-visited test set. The base train-volume range already misses the independently measured NPT equilibrium (§6.70); investigate coverage, do not relabel it an optimizer diagnosis.

All 30 validation volume-equivalent cell edges lie **inside** the full training range (15.4950–15.7605 Å). Missing the NPT equilibrium cannot by itself explain the validation error as out-of-range volume. This check does not establish coverage of shear, species environments, or student-visited states.

**Batch 4 is not four tiny graphs.** The stock student uses 320 atoms/frame, 5 Å cutoff, 8 Bessels, angular degrees through 3, two interactions, scalar hidden irreps and correlation 3. The CPU census of the frozen SrTiO₃ extxyz files used the stock `matscipy` directed `ijS` convention, excluding only zero-image self edges. Counts and file identities are in [STATUS §6.70](../STATUS_AND_PROFILE.md). The estimates below are payload sizes, **not GPU measurements**.

| Frozen train frames | Directed edges, total | Median per frame | Just harmonic values + Cartesian Jacobians, FP64 | Full suggested basis cache, FP64 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 | 4,651,748 | 15,574 | 2.22 GiB | 2.98 GiB |
| 3,000 | 44,469,794 | 14,893 | 21.20 GiB | 28.49 GiB |

There are 16 harmonic values and 48 Cartesian partials per edge. Add 8 radial values, 8 radial derivatives and two 3-vectors: **86 scalars × 8 bytes**. Even the 3,000-frame full payload alone exceeds the [24 GB L4](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/l4/), before graph indices, model, saved activations, optimizer and workspace. FP32 halves payload, not necessarily epoch time. Coordinates/labels alone, integer graph connectivity, and an all-derivatives cache are three different proposals. Batch 4 already has about 62k directed edges on the base data; a 32k–64k edge budget fits roughly 2–4 frames, **not 16–32**. A prepacked GPU dataset still needs gathers/reindexing to construct shuffled batches. Host staging, a bounded device working set, and compute-on-demand are valid comparators.

In that layout the two vectors are the unit direction and the Cartesian displacement for strain. If the latter is reconstructed from a stored **scalar length** instead, the count is 84 rather than 86: 2.91/27.83 GiB at 300/3,000 frames. Neither count adds separate cutoff scalars to the cutoff-applied radial basis.

### What the literature actually timed

- **Closest positive precedent:** [NVIDIA's cuEquivariance training measurements, Fig. 3](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerate-drug-and-material-discovery-with-new-math-library-nvidia-cuequivariance/) report 5.9× on MACE-MP Large and 6.1× on MACE-OFF Large, FP64 A100 batch 32. Good reason to benchmark the library. Different irreps, hardware, and batch; not an estimate for our student. [Benoit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23621) reports substantial block dispatch costs, ~3× cuEq **inference**, and degraded toy training with half-precision weights. Neither the limiting block nor a 4–12× FP32 training gain follows.
- [OpenEquivariance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13986): up to 6.2× **FP64 MACE inference** over original, not stock stress training. [FlashTP, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/lee25l.html): real **SevenNet** training evidence, 76→22 min/epoch on A100. Its channelwise path is relevant; not a ready-made MACE trainer. [FlashTP issue 11](https://github.com/SNU-ARC/flashTP/issues/11) reports wrong outputs on stream/capture paths; qualify increasing derivatives, dtype and non-default streams at a specific commit before considering integration.
- [Sobek](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18074): 1.2–49.7× for **operators** across shapes and differentiation orders. The top figure is not the homogeneous channelwise double backward. [Implementation](https://github.com/Cortex-Discovery/sobek): check FP32/Python constraints; not an FP64 reference replacement. [TrimSO(2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20029) benchmarks SO(2) architectures (eSEN, UMA, EquiformerV3), not the scalar-sender MACE interaction. [e3j](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35099) is JAX; its MACE +34% is water-box **NPT simulation**, not training. These support testing intermediate elimination, not changing runtime ecosystems by default.
- [DeePMD-GNN's training experiment](https://github.com/deepmodeling/deepmd-gnn#training-mace-with-torchcompile): 1.36× steady state on RTX 5090 water at batch 1 in its own stack, with large startup cost; not a stock CLI switch. [MACE discussion 1768](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/discussions/1768) concerns a modified MACE-MH-1. [DPA4, Table S-3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02419v4#S3.T3) separates 1.61× compiled FP32 from 3.1× **including BF16 AMP**. [MACE-ICTC](https://github.com/Parity-LRX/MACE-ICTC) is an existing Cartesian-basis training and conversion implementation worth studying before building one; a changed basis is not automatically Adam-equivalent.
- [cuEq 0.13 release notes](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cuEquivariance/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md#0130-2026-10-15): the quoted 1.9× memory/capture warning is for **triangle multiplicative update**, not a blanket MACE TP restriction. Conversely, successful inference capture is not training proof. [PyTorch graphed callables](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.cuda.make_graphed_callables.html) exclude higher-order differentiation. For manual whole-step capture: static storage and current-stream discipline, real changing labels/geometry/optimizer state, multiple replays, raw gradient/update checks, capture pools and phase-transition costs. Try the compiler without capture first.
- [cuBLAS Ozaki-II](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/faster-scientific-simulations-with-new-fp64-algorithms-in-nvidia-cublas/): vendor results of 3.9×/5.5× are **DGEMM on GB200/Rubin**. The actual L4 log reports CUDA 13.0, not the cited 13.4.2. Only revisit if FP64 survives, eligible dense GEMMs dominate, and the installed cuBLAS/device/shapes dispatch the path. The repo's 3×TF32/FP16 cubins approximate **FP32**, not FP64; do not substitute them into an FP64 parity arm.

## Exact candidates if something still dominates

1. **Frozen geometry, in increasing order of cost.** Start with static topology and a per-frame fixed **scaled** ZBL triplet (energy, forces, stress). A detached energy alone loses F and stress. Keep raw E0/force RMS/neighbor statistics; add back the triplet for physical predictions. Test non-unit scale, active core, zero net atomic force with nonzero periodic stress. Next cache only radial values and first derivatives. For fixed coordinates/strain `q₀` and fixed features `z`, the local surrogate `z(q₀)+J(q₀)(q−q₀)` preserves energy, first spatial derivatives, and their parameter gradients **at q₀**. It does not preserve a true spatial Hessian or updated geometry. Contract per-edge partials with endpoint differences and image-aware strain; keep the adjoint differentiable. Compare compact, batch-local angular recomputation versus full storage, including transfer and saved-tensor lifetime. [sphericart](https://sphericart.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pytorch-api.html) provides Cartesian derivatives; match e3nn component normalization, axis order, normalized rather than solid harmonics, and all degrees before timing. Invalidate caches on trainable bases, transformed distances, rotations/rattles or cutoff changes as applicable.
2. **Share only the radial work across reverse edges.** Match the full multiset `(s,t,n) ↔ (t,s,−n)` within each frame; retain both directed messages, distinct images, and both incoming adjoints. For distance-only radial weights, use one MLP row per pair **per layer**. Angular parity is `Y_l(−d)=(−1)^l Y_l(d)` but its Cartesian Jacobian has sign `(−1)^(l+1)`. No sharing for asymmetric species-conditioned inputs without proof. Test mixed species, odd harmonics, shear, nonzero self-images, the cutoff and unequal endpoint adjoints. Halving MLP rows doesn't halve the rest of the step; measure gathers and reverse reductions as well as GEMM time.
3. **Compare two message schedules and the product block.** If TP workspace/traffic dominates, try consuming the last radial projection directly in a receiver-grouped scalar-sender contraction; compare to the simpler materialized-weight reference. Under `l_in=0` the angular factor multiplies rather than performs a general CG sum. Log per-layer shapes, not an assumed identical signature. Receiver CSR and sender CSC can remove atomics for their node sums, not automatically for weight/strain/species reductions. Charge permutations and require repeatability separately from numerical parity. If the symmetric product dominates instead, try library contraction, then species grouping with inverse permutation and fixed CG factors. [Vendored generator](../loupmd/export/_vendor/fastmace/kernels/cuda/sc_factored.py) folds weights and emits an inference reverse pass; keep the basis map on tape and canonical optimizer coordinates. Do not edit the vendored generator to add a trainer.
4. **Try reverse-over-forward as a measured alternative, not a free second derivative.** Compute exact current E/F/stress, detach loss sensitivities, then evaluate the design's contracted energy plus geometry JVP and reverse into parameters. A joint scalar-energy VJP already returns **all** force/stress components in one reverse sweep. `create_graph=False` does not remove the first forward's saves; reversing the tangent program needs derivatives of its JVP, including activation second derivatives (e.g. SiLU). An opaque native JVP needs that reverse rule. Compare recomputation and checkpointing selected blocks to the ordinary `create_graph=True` implementation first. Reject if the two-pass path only wins by evaluating residuals with approximated inference weights or by ignoring the extra pass, live tensors or memory. [PyTorch transform contract](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/extending.func.html).

## Ideas that change what learns (Stage 1 trials, not faster equivalent steps)

Rank by the failure observed on a control run. Compare best qualified models at equal allocated CU as well as CU to the same target. Count teacher labelling, failed trials and tuning; keep the baseline's second seed and relevant non-oxide systems. None of these numbers predicts an S64 speedup.

| Candidate | Primary evidence and experiment |
|---|---|
| **Centered teacher site energies** | Already in [Loup-S §7/§10](../LOUP_S_DESIGN.md), not a new engine feature. [SevenNet-Nano](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01103) uses site labels **alongside** total E/F/stress. [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2026.109742) report improved *energy* accuracy from atomic-energy alignment on top of Hessian distillation. Teacher partitions are not unique physical observables. Check atom mapping and the sum to the physical total; align head, species means, offsets and ZBL conventions before centering on train only. Compare frozen trunk fine-tuning with/without the site term at matching updates. This loss adds supervision; it does not make the force backward disappear. |
| **Optimizer or batch change** | [Harari et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02499v1) report SOAP reached AdamW's minimum median validation-force MAE 4.9×/5.8× sooner in particular Allegro/NequIP experiments, not MACE with stress. Start with a learning-rate/clipping diagnostic and then test SOAP alone, full labels, versus tuned reference Adam+AMSGrad. Map flattened e3nn parameters to the intended matrices; charge preconditioner cost/memory. SOAP-Muon, schedule-free AdamW, cosine/warmup, and a larger effective batch with an edge budget each change the optimizer trajectory. Test independently; compare optimizer steps **and** epochs. |
| **Partial derivative labels** | Harari's 5% result is not a guarantee for water or SrTiO₃ stress. Select whole **training** frames for E-only versus E/F/stress computation; mask-only after computing full forces saves no derivative work. Specify sampling probabilities and loss denominators, check per-species/tail/pressure on full validation labels, restore full conservative loss for final qualification. An isotropic random projection of a force residual can estimate its squared norm after normalization, but fewer projections introduce variance; differentiating the directional energy w.r.t. weights still uses a mixed derivative. This is not the exact residual contraction in [Smith et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05475v1#S2.SS3). That paper's finite-difference method is an oracle, not exact production arithmetic. |
| **Curvature only if the spectrum needs it** | [Amin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09009) show Hessian-row distillation can help small students; 20× is student **inference relative to teacher**. [PFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07742) targets phonon observables with sampled force constants. [Projected Hessian Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04523) reports >24× vs a *full Hessian* on small molecules, not vs E/F training. Prefer the design's paired finite-displacement forces for the first ablation: two teacher labels and two disjoint student force graphs, opposite sensitivities. Each geometry needs its own bases and correct neighbor list (or a validated superset with correct masks); a small displacement can cross the cutoff. Exact student HVP supervision instead requires a third mixed derivative during parameter optimization. |
| **Direct-force warm-up and latent matching** | [Bigi, Langer & Ceriotti](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11569v3#A8) obtained similar force accuracy with about one-third of the reference compute in PET experiments; no transfer proof for S64 or a direct-stress head. An equivariant **linear** map from `64×0e` to `1o` is identically zero. Hydrostatic stress is scalar but anisotropic symmetric stress also needs `2e`. One could tap intermediate angular features or couple geometry into a new temporary head; price it as a new architecture and qualify the final conservative energy, stress and MD. [Hidden-feature distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14818) motivates a cheaper scalar-feature auxiliary: compare matching irreps with a learned projection, measure teacher extraction/storage, discard projector for release. |
| **Curriculum, sampling and warm starts** | [Electrolyte distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39513) used E/F/stress and teacher-relabeled rattles, not teacher atomic-energy labels. [Entropy-based selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20384) concerns data selection, not guaranteed savings for online worst-error mining. Compare diverse, trajectory-disjoint training against a mixture of hard and representative frames; preserve equilibrium coverage. Oversampling changes the objective unless correctly reweighted. Keep learning-curve fits cold, use the exact pre-phase-two parent for closed-loop branches, compare same-trunk warm starts to a default with equal extra updates. No presumed 2–3× from SVD truncation or successive halving: retain full-run controls for the early ranking. |

## When to build an owned kernel

Only after a valid fit and the cheapest qualified baseline exist. Capture one trace in each phase without changing ongoing Stage 0 runs. Attribute wall time separately to geometry, radial projection, message passing, symmetric product, force/strain derivative, outer parameter backward, loader, validation and saving. Use the slowest **replaceable wall fraction**, not the largest sum of overlapping kernel durations. For example, a kernel covering 20% of the epoch and running 3× faster yields at most **1.15×** for the epoch; that misses the proposed 1.20× investigation screen before integration overhead. On the design's estimated 30–45 GPU-h campaign, no fixed 300–500 GPU-h cutoff is necessary: price expected reuse, engineering time, actual covered shapes, qualification and maintenance. A capacity win that enables an otherwise impossible fit has independent value; say so rather than claiming it as measured speed.

Promote **one** candidate per bottleneck: launch-bound → compile or legal batching; TP traffic → radial pairing/streaming; product-bound → qualified symmetric contraction; retained activations → checkpoint versus JVP strategy. If all reference runs miss §4, diagnose data/model/recipe rather than report a time-to-target ratio. With no qualified improvement, ship no custom backend and proceed on mace-torch.

## Follow-on audit: the proposed blueprints and six-probe script

**Reject the scalar-only shortcut for both fixtures.** The two seed-1 logs say `Hidden irreps: 64x0e` **and** “correlation order: 3 (body order: 4) and spherical harmonics up to: l=3.” Angular messages built from a scalar sender still have 16 harmonic components; their contractions can produce scalar node outputs without discarding angular information. Replacing the whole product by powers of one scalar per channel changes the learned potential. Even for a distinct, truly `max_ell=0` ablation, component-normalized e3nn uses `Y00 = 1`, not the integral-normalized `1/sqrt(4π)`. The two epoch times differ with radial MLP widths; they do not measure “wasted” equivariance.

**Try existing tracing support before rewriting e3nn.** In 0.4.4 `Linear`/`TensorProduct` are Python modules with generated children that can be scripted; `SphericalHarmonics` calls a scripted function. A compile annotation does not mean every class is itself a `ScriptModule` or every boundary breaks. Pinned [MACE `prepare(factory)`](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/blob/v0.3.16/mace/tools/compile.py) can construct with `jit_script_fx=False` and trace selected modules. Restore canonical weights into that construction; toggling a flag on an already-built model does not recreate its children. Measure graph breaks, actual kernels and mixed gradients on the chosen trace boundary. AOT packaging and CUDA Graph replay do not themselves fuse operators or promise cache residence.

**Fold only truly adjacent maps.** Two square width-`C` maps on `N` rows cost approximately `2 N C²` multiply-adds versus `N C² + C³` for a live weight product (forward only). With `N=1,280`, `C=64`, that is 1.90× less arithmetic *for those maps*, not for the full step. In the [pinned blocks](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/blob/v0.3.16/mace/modules/blocks.py), the species embedding followed by first-hop `linear_up` can use one normalized table constructed on tape; first-hop post-aggregation message linear/neighbor normalization followed by its species skip is a second candidate. The residual-hop skip uses **pre-projection** features, a different branch; never push a fold through an activation, nonlinear product or fan-out without accounting for the other consumers. Preserve parameter groups, weight decay, biases/path normalization and gradients; recompute on optimizer updates and EMA swaps (`EMA(W₁W₂)` need not equal `EMA(W₁)EMA(W₂)`). Not bitwise; compare warm updates and measured saved bytes. The [stock product](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/blob/v0.3.16/mace/modules/symmetric_contraction.py) already nests body orders in a Horner-like recurrence: optimize the schedule of that recurrence, not a fictional unrolled baseline.

**Separate physical validation batches from logical metric batches.** On one frozen EMA snapshot, larger disjoint physical batches can be split back to original frame order, `ptr`, node forces and stress for groups **8/8/8/6**. The scheduler needs the sum of these *four reduced losses* divided by 30, not one reduced loss divided by 30. A CPU replay on the first 30 scored teacher-test predictions, using phase-one weights solely as a metric fixture, gives **0.09041890 vs 0.02261982** respectively. Check all metrics, not just loss, and plateau decisions; different GPU batching can round differently. Cannot do this across successive *training* optimizer updates. Try the cheaper change first: [MACE validation](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/blob/v0.3.16/mace/tools/train.py) discards the per-batch `metrics(...)` result; compare `metrics.update(...)` per original batch + one final `compute()` with the [installed TorchMetrics forwarding behavior](https://github.com/Lightning-AI/torchmetrics/blob/v1.9.0/src/torchmetrics/metric.py). Actual training host read is `to_numpy(loss)` (`.cpu().detach().numpy()`), not necessarily a device-wide `.item()` barrier. Buffer detached per-step log values; record with a single ordered transfer if the trace justifies it. Python EMA loops dispatch tensor operations, not one host sync per parameter. Also test known real-atom `output_size` for loss `repeat_interleave` to avoid shape discovery; do not change padding denominators or per-step log semantics.

**Blueprint A: use a physical-coordinate bridge, not an invented backward.** A function whose forward ignores `δr,ε` and returns constants has zero numerical coordinate derivative. Implement the *local affine function* of those arguments, or expose an explicit VJP in feature space and test its physical-coordinate contractions independently. Given a reverse pair, `r_e(q)=r_rev(q)` implies their length Jacobians w.r.t. physical positions/strain are identical. Add the two generally unequal radial cotangents, apply the sum **once** on a canonical pair, and scatter physical forces and stress. Equivalently half of the sum on each direction gives the same *physical* quantities, not the true derivatives w.r.t. two independently variable edge vectors. Never half each original cotangent or scatter the summed one twice. Keep directional angular adjoints; reverse harmonic values use `(-1)^l` and Cartesian Jacobians `(-1)^(l+1)`. Half-storage is possible; half the DRAM traffic is not assured if the two receiver rows read the pair separately.

**A smaller first cache trial is feasible on paper.** A CPU multiset check on **every** 5 Å graph in `curve-3000/train.xyz` found 44,469,794 directed rows matched by `(s,t,n) ↔ (t,s,−n)`: 22,234,897 pair rows. Store eight cutoff-applied radial values, eight radial derivatives, length and unit vector (20 doubles per pair), recompute angular terms: **3.31 GiB payload + 0.166 GiB** for one int32 pair ID per directed edge. Excludes topology, orientation bookkeeping, activations, optimizer, and transfers; compare with caching ZBL alone. Even **only** 27 FP32 `dY/dd` values per directed edge at a hypothetical `l=2` would be 479 MiB on the same 300-frame graphs, not <15 MB. No measured device-memory or epoch-speed claim.

**Blueprint B: verified identity, not verified speed.** CPU FP64 check used a skew cell, a periodic self-image, asymmetric directed edge weights, a nonlinear radial/angular energy and nonzero shear targets. Compared the direct E/F/stress loss derivative using reverse-over-reverse, the canonical-pair affine bridge, and reverse over a detached-residual geometry JVP. Both alternatives differed from the direct parameter gradient by at most `8.9e−16`. Self-image positional derivative was zero while its maximum strain derivative was `0.827`. The affine fixture passed `gradcheck`/`gradgradcheck` for its own function; the constant-forward/invented-backward control failed. The affine and original **spatial Hessians** differed by as much as `6.30`, as expected. This is not a stock MACE equivalence, memory, capture, or runtime test. Native live-weight residuals and a differentiable tangent rule for every nonlinear block are still prerequisites.

**The suggested six probes do not fit the cap.** Five epochs of *just FP64* for both students at the observed phase-one medians take `5 × (48.6 + 43.5) = 460.5 s` (>7.6 min), before FP32, TF32, installation or any second card. Replaying epochs 25→100 costs about 61 min at 48.6 s/epoch; saved model checkpoints do not contain historical per-step gradient norms. Five minutes of A100 alone would add ~0.56 CU. A three-step trace can suggest a bottleneck, not certify time to fidelity or prove graph capture. The [30-minute first probe](loup-s-training-engine.md) is therefore **one card, one shape, one baseline**. Afterwards use the trace to choose between validation/host work, library dispatch, radial pairing, symmetric products, or further precision/hardware comparisons. Full-schedule precision, second seed, both-order speed tests and larger datasets remain separate qualification.

[Pinned MACE](https://github.com/ACEsuit/mace/blob/v0.3.16/mace/cli/run_train.py) actually has an L-BFGS path: strong Wolfe, closure over minibatches. Full-objective gradients need not fit 4.65M edges on the GPU at once. Count repeated closure evaluations, optimizer history, clipping effects on the search direction and new scheduler/checkpoint behavior. [Multistream Kalman training](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.8b01092) concerns Behler potentials; [water comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03769) illustrates that fewer updates are not equal wall-time speedups. Defer quadratic parameter covariance; test SOAP independently before combining any optimizers. |
| 32–33: phase ramp, stress subsampling | The validation-force deterioration predates epoch 111. An AMSGrad maximum is not invalid merely because the loss changes. Clone **before** the first changed epoch and test just the weight ramp against unchanged LR/optimizer; moment resets and cosine LR are separate interventions. `1:100` weights do not imply stress costs 1% of force or contributes 1% of the gradient. Position and strain use one joint VJP. Independent random stress inclusion `I` with probability `p` and `I L_S / p` preserves the expected *unclipped* gradient at fixed weights, not the Adam trajectory; deterministic every-fourth-step does not have that property per step. Drop the actual strain/virial path before claiming savings. Keep full pressure/shear and NPT qualification. |
| 34–37: direct heads, projected forces, atomic energies, curvature | Keep the controls above. Source site labels from teacher evaluation, not `harvest.py`, which only selects frames.
