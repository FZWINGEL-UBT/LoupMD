## 1. Recover the small-width speed that the existing checkpoints leave on the table

At C16 the two edge stages take 325.9 and 315.3 µs of the approximately 943 µs profiled kernel sum. Moving the unprofiled 1,004 µs step under 750 µs, the 0.15 µs/atom-step target, requires saving about 254 µs. As a rough budget, halving the sum of both edge stages would remove 321 µs of profiled work. Profiled sums include overlap and instrumentation effects, so use whole-step timings to test the budget. [Kernel census](PHASE2_DISTILL_RESULTS.md) · [Speed bar](LOUP_S_DESIGN.md#4-contract)

- **Layer 0, fixed small radial width.** Specialize for the exported learned-column count `J - 1 = 9`, even though the model is called R8. Try a 16-lane group with one radial column per lane and two independent edge chunks per warp in the backward. Keep the derivative and force/virial assembly parallel across edges. In the forward, try distributing the harmonic accumulation across the spare lanes or packing two independent receiver rows, and charge the cost of staging ragged species segments. Preserve the density column, pair lookup, SH permutation and ZBL path when present. The previous half-warp trial tied at `J = 65`. The much narrower live set is the reason to retest this shape. [Layer-0 kernels](src/layer0_kernels.cuh) · [Prior trial](OPTIMISATION_LEDGER.md#ideas-20261121-list10)
- **Layer 1, pack the C16 work.** Try two 16-lane groups for separate receiver rows in the scalar-sender path, with per-group reductions and edge metadata. Compare same-row edge parallelism if different degree counts leave one group idle. Keep the C32 launch as a timing control. Inspect registers, table loads, edge metadata and reverse gathers before deciding whether to carry the schedule into product kernels.
- **Node maps.** After the edge comparison, pick one adjacent projection/product boundary from the trace. Benchmark a small grouped GEMM or fused readout against the existing generated kernels and graph-replayed GEMMs on identical inputs. Preserve the nonlinear readout and the product's species weights. A smaller kernel time is useful only if the win survives the full graph. [Convolution generator](codegen/gen_generic.py) · [Product generator](loupmd/export/gen_product.py)

For these exact execution experiments, compare the same bundles, including C16 and C32 on SrTiO₃, at 320 and 5,000 atoms. Check the original 3× E/F/stress gate on forced variants and variable-cell paths before interpreting MD results. Use the repository's quick funnel, followed by sustained A/B/B/A in both build orders on the 5060 Ti and RTX 4000 Ada. [Benchmark workflow](AGENTS.md#4-benchmarking--profiling-discipline)

## 2. Distil a narrow second hop while keeping a wide local energy

The design already proposes narrow `linear_up`, one-layer controls and a factorized radial table. The proposed addition is to keep two-hop information **linear in a compact transported field**. A one-layer model is the no-transport control. Compared with a full second product block, this restriction puts most many-body interactions on the receiver and makes the transport cost explicit. [Existing architecture ablations](LOUP_S_DESIGN.md#52-defaults-and-their-ablations) · [Existing factorized-message proposal](LOUP_S_DESIGN.md#12-beyond-loup-s)

Let `A_i` be the first-hop density, `h_j` a sender feature derived from that density and `Q_i[a,l]` a receiver field derived from local projections of `A_i`. A scalar sender mode gives

```text
g_j[a]     = U[a] h_j
M_i[a,l]   = sum_j g_j[a] phi_a(r_ij) Y_l(r̂_ij)
E_i        = E_local(A_i) + sum_(a,l) <Q_i[a,l], M_i[a,l]>
```

Build `Q` from one local factor or an exact CG projection of two local factors. Each inner product joins fields in the same O(3) irrep, including parity. One radial profile per transported mode reduces the aggregate to `R_transport × (L+1)^2`, without forming a radial-by-mode product or an edge-by-probe buffer. The local cubic can use the same two receiver factors in selected ranks. The model omits terms containing two or more transported fields. Those terms may matter, so train the restriction and compare it directly with the full student.

**SrTiO₃.** At 5 Å, compare (a) one local cubic block with the nonlinear readout, (b) the same block with 4 or 8 scalar transport modes, and (c) the existing two-hop C16 and C32. Leave the first-hop angular span intact. Screen force, energy and stress on the same splits, then compare soft modes and strain probes on the validation set. Use the full 960-mode and NPT/NVE checks to qualify a finalist. A short-contact error at low aggregate target RMSE is a separate failure mode. For that case, compare a small fit on physically sampled short-contact/strain frames, with the core and main recipe fixed, before adding channels.

**Salt.** Keep the C32 local block and compare transport with 8 and 16 vector modes against the equal-width vector control. First use the current exporter-supported layout wherever a constrained projection fits it. Then try one explicit bond-bound term from `v_j · r̂_ij` and a vector path that preserves transverse information. A vector sender generalizes the formula by replacing `g_j Y_l` with a parity-correct CG projection of `v_j ⊗ Y_l`. Keep the 6 Å cutoff in the first comparison. Use the C64 diffusion-passing model as the physics control. If only specific species fail, diagnose self-diffusion alongside coordination and residence times on the same trajectories, then add independent teacher-relabeled environments for those failures. Keep the original NPT and 1800/2160 K NVT evaluations independent.

**Dual cutoff after the width trial.** Compare a locally dense inner range plus a narrow scalar/vector shell against the same model with a single 6 Å cutoff. Keep a common superset list, with separate active rows only if the implementation can skip the expensive outer work. Use smooth envelopes with zero first and second derivatives at the joins and include the shell in forces and strain stress. Report total list and active edge counts. The design already proposes dual cutoffs; the question here is whether a **bond-bound** shell recovers the fidelity of the full transport at fixed cost. [Dual-cutoff controls](LOUP_S_DESIGN.md#52-defaults-and-their-ablations)

## 3. Make the water objective sensitive to intermolecular errors before compressing it

The stock distillation path already implements net-force and COM-torque loss. Port that definition to the fast vector-student path if the selected runner lacks it. The native CLI currently exposes E/F/stress, the site-energy term and VarPro. The Phase 2 water rows change multiple variables at once, so they cannot isolate radial width, cutoff, repulsion or objective. [Stock molecular loss](loupmd/distill/molecular_loss.py) · [Native loss interface](include/loupstrain.h) · [Native CLI](src/train/loupstrain_cli.cu) · [Water protocol](loupmd/distill/water_protocol.py)

1. Freeze the 6 Å, R64, ZBL-off architecture, data, centering and initialization. Compare E/F/stress, then the same loss plus molecular force and torque. Match the current site-energy weight and decay in both arms. Normalize molecular terms using train-only reference RMS scales. For a molecule with unwrapped COM arms `rho_i`, the force sensitivities from net force and torque add `q_net + q_torque × rho_i` at atom `i`, with the loss normalization included in the two cotangents. This fits the existing mixed-derivative schedule and needs no additional teacher calls. Check grouped PBC indexing and parity against `MolecularStressLoss`, including a molecule crossing the cell boundary and a batch of different-sized configurations.
2. After that comparison, test whether the 64 radial profiles are needed for all paths. Sample value and derivative matrices of the trained radial table over representative bonds, separately for O–H covalent, intermolecular H-bond and outer-shell intervals. Weight derivatives in physical units using the actual force sensitivities. Use the spectra as a screen, then train a compact shared span for the transported paths while preserving the full local radial bank. Compare R16/R32 with R64 at the same cutoff and objective. On export, check the interpolated radial values and derivatives in the short-contact and switching regions.
3. Select by atomic and species force RMSE **and** the separate 2% molecular force/torque criteria. Run matched density, partial RDFs, O diffusion and 0.5 fs NVE after that screen. Test at 648 and 5,184 atoms on the same GPU. If a model still misses torque with strong atomic force scores, use teacher-relabeled molecular rotations and H-bond distortions as a data ablation before widening the entire architecture.

## 4. Fit missing physics with the cheapest supervision that changes it

- **Curvature targeted at a failed mode.** Loup-S already has a paired-displacement stock training path. Use it only after the locked validation set identifies failure in soft oxide modes, elastic response, or a molecular mode. Add opposite displacements along the identified directions with a separate fixed evaluation set. Compare against the same number of matched non-targeted force labels. Rebuild the neighbor list for each displacement, verify convergence at two step sizes and report both teacher-label cost and training time. [Paired displacement trainer](loupmd/distill/curvature_train.py)
- **Include forces and stress in the existing VarPro polish.** The frozen trunk makes E, F and stress linear in the first readout and the last linear layer. The current solve uses only frame energies. Compare no polish, the existing energy-only solve, and a ridge update around the same weights on energy, selected force directions and all independent stress components. Start with a tiny deterministic subset and use features differentiated in Torch as an oracle. Accumulate normal equations in float64 or use a QR solve if conditioning dominates. Report projected-design rank and regularization along with any change in force, pressure and phonon error. Check that the conservative model changes exactly as predicted by the readout update. [Current solve](src/train/loupstrain_cli.cu)
- **Use cheaply trained students to choose teacher frames.** Test an equal-label-budget comparison between representative frames alone and representative frames plus new, disjoint student-visited hard frames. Compare on the same common validation trajectories and use an independent student-visited replay for final qualification. Preserve species, temperature and stress coverage when choosing hard frames. Track teacher calls, wall time and qualified checkpoints. Keep the current optimizer schedule and use the existing loop and fidelity reports. [Harvest](loupmd/distill/harvest.py) · [Fidelity](loupmd/distill/fidelity.py)

## Acceptance for the first pass

- Freeze the teacher/head, split digests, seed list, optimizer, training budget, neighbor convention, export settings and reference checkpoints. Vary one model or objective choice per arm. Start unsupported architectures with a differentiable reference and check rotational, reflection, translation and permutation behavior, energy-derived forces and strain stress before specializing the native trainer. Check mixed coordinate/parameter derivatives on any new training primitive.
- Compare architecture candidates at equal training budget and at equal attained fidelity. Report native inference, complete MD steps, final model quality, and cold wall time to a qualified artifact separately. Keep all acceptance metrics from the Loup-S contract, including species errors and force tails. Use paired MD seeds and uncertainty for diffusion and density. Treat passing a single short run as a screening result.
- An already-trained C16 SrTiO₃ bundle gives a cheap timing decision. The water objective and salt vector-width screens give cheap capacity decisions. Build transport kernels after one of those capacity screens survives, and time the backward, stress and rebuild paths along with forward. Retain the student's own float64 precision gate alongside independent teacher fidelity.
